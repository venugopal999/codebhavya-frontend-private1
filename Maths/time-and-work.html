⏱️ Time & Work

Relate work, time and efficiency; combine individual rates; distribute wages; and solve worker, pipe and cistern problems.

🎯 1. Learning Objectives

  • Express one-day work as a work rate.
  • Find individual and combined completion times.
  • Compare efficiency and time ratios.
  • Solve workers–days–hours problems.
  • Divide wages according to work completed.
  • Handle pipes, leaks and alternate-day work.

📖 2. Work, Time and Rate

Total Work

Treat one complete job as 1 unit. Fractions then represent completed or remaining work.

Work Rate

If a person completes a job in n days, the person completes 1/n of the job per day.

Time

Time = Work ÷ Rate

Work Done

Work = Rate × Time
Key idea: add rates when people or pipes work together. Do not add their completion times.

👤 3. Individual Work

One-Day Work

If time = n days, rate = 1/n

Work in d Days

Work done = d/n

Remaining Work

Remaining = 1 − completed work

Time for a Fraction

Time = required fraction ÷ rate

🤝 4. Combined Work

If A finishes in a days and B in b days, their rates are 1/a and 1/b.

Combined Rate

1/a + 1/b

Combined Time

ab/(a+b)

Find B from A+B

B's rate = combined rate − A's rate

Three Workers

Rate = 1/a + 1/b + 1/c

⚡ 5. Efficiency and Time

Inverse Relationship

For the same work, a more efficient person requires less time.

Efficiency ∝ 1/Time

Ratio Rule

If efficiencies A:B = m:n, then their completion times are reversed.

Time A:Time B = n:m
Example: A is twice as efficient as B, so A needs half of B's time.

👷 6. Workers, Days and Hours

For equally efficient workers and fixed work:

Workers × Days × Hours per day = Constant
Change Effect on Time Relationship
More workers Fewer days Inverse
More hours per day Fewer days Inverse
More work More time Direct
Higher efficiency Less time Inverse

💵 7. Work and Wages

Wages are divided in the ratio of work completed. When working for the same duration, work is proportional to efficiency.

Same Duration

Wages ratio = efficiency ratio

Different Durations

Wages ratio = rate × time

🚰 8. Pipes and Cisterns

Inlet Pipe

A filling pipe contributes a positive rate.

Rate = +1/filling time

Outlet or Leak

An emptying pipe contributes a negative rate.

Rate = −1/emptying time

Net Rate

Sum of inlet rates − sum of outlet rates

Filling Time

Time = 1 ÷ net rate

🔁 9. Workers Joining, Leaving and Alternating

Join or Leave

Split the timeline into stages. Find work completed in each stage using the active combined rate.

Alternate Days

Calculate one full cycle first, count complete cycles, then handle the remaining fractional day.

🧾 11. Solved Examples

Example 1: Together

Problem: A takes 10 days and B takes 15 days.

Solution: Rate=1/10+1/15=1/6. Together they take 6 days.

Example 2: Find a Partner

Problem: A+B take 6 days; A alone takes 10 days.

Solution: B's rate=1/6−1/10=1/15. B takes 15 days.

Example 3: Workers

Problem: 8 workers finish in 15 days. Find time for 12 workers.

Solution: 8×15=12×d, so d=10 days.

Example 4: Pipe and Leak

Problem: Inlet fills in 8 h; outlet empties in 24 h.

Solution: Net rate=1/8−1/24=1/12. Tank fills in 12 hours.

💻 12. Applications in Programming

Task Scheduling

Estimate when parallel workers or processors will complete a collection of jobs.

Load Balancing

Assign work according to processor speed or worker efficiency.

Project Planning

Calculate team size, completion time and productivity changes.

Network Flow

Model positive input rates, negative output rates and net throughput.

⚠️ 13. Common Mistakes

Adding Times

Add work rates, not the separate completion times.

Ignoring Inverse Ratios

Higher efficiency means lower time for the same work.

Treating an Outlet as Positive

A leak or outlet subtracts from the filling rate.

Skipping Stages

When workers join or leave, calculate every time interval separately.

✍️ 14. Practice Problems

Solve each problem first, then use Show Solution.

1. A completes a job in 12 days. What work does A complete in 3 days?

Work=3×1/12=1/4.

2. A takes 8 days and B takes 24 days. Find their combined time.

Rate=1/8+1/24=1/6, so time=6 days.

3. Efficiencies A:B=4:5. Find their time ratio.

Time ratio is the inverse: 5:4.

4. 10 workers finish a job in 18 days. How many days will 15 workers need?

10×18=15×d, so d=12 days.

5. An inlet fills a tank in 6 hours and a leak empties it in 12 hours. Find the net filling time.

Net rate=1/6−1/12=1/12, so time=12 hours.

📝 15. Quick Revision

  • One-day work = 1/completion time.
  • Combined rate is the sum of active rates.
  • Efficiency ratio is the inverse of time ratio.
  • Workers × days × hours is constant for fixed work.
  • Wages follow the ratio of completed work.
  • Inlets are positive; outlets and leaks are negative.
